## Title

**An Open, Computer-Controlled Vacuum Induction Furnace for Accelerated
Grain-Growth Processing of Metals and Ceramics**

## Abstract (150 words)

Microstructure-sensitive design and grain-boundary studies need specimens with
systematically varied grain structures, conventionally produced by long
near-melting-point anneals in costly turnkey vacuum induction furnaces. We
present an open retrofit stack converting a bare commercial RF induction
generator into a computer-controlled, vacuum-integrated annealing system:
LabVIEW/DAQ analog power control, dual-wavelength pyrometer feedback, a
high-vacuum quartz-tube chamber, and an in-house machined graphite susceptor.
Requiring only a monotonic analog power input, the stack transfers across
generators; the reference build uses a 6 kW solid-state unit.
Power–temperature calibration achieved R² = 0.991 over 1200–1400 °C; eight
12-hour nickel anneals reproduced a 1200 °C soak within ±1.3 °C, and 40-hour
soaks at 1325 °C remained stable. Annealed microstructures were validated by
optical metallography, SEM, and EBSD. A modified charge stack reaches
2500 °C, coarsening YSZ grains from 10 to 90 µm in 45 minutes versus 228 hours
conventionally. Design files, software, and data are openly available.

## Symposium fit

- **Advanced characterization:** EBSD/Kikuchi-pattern validation of
  furnace-annealed nickel; thermal-grooving grain maps.
- **Microstructure-sensitive design:** the furnace is an enabling capability
  for building specimen libraries with controlled grain-size distributions
  (Ni 1200–1400 °C anneals; YSZ 10 → 90 µm coarsening).
- BYU connection: last author Oliver Johnson is a symposium organizer;
  the honoree, David T. Fullwood, is BYU faculty.
